# Bounded example acquisition experiment v2

Teacher examples supply only text, authored graph and source spans. One character-span graph grammar parses both passage and question. No language-specific lexical list, morphology, tokenizer, regex or hand-authored language rules are used. Candidates and fragment holes arise from observed examples; search bounds and costs describe the explored space only. IDs and expected pair answers are excluded from grammar input. Parsing preserves all candidate graphs and explicitly reports ambiguity, unknowns and limits.

Admission requires disjoint train/dev, unique full graph derivations, no prior correct-dev regression, person/role/negation/source perturbation coverage, and actual execution of predicted passage and question graphs through the existing native executor. Answer tuples, exact graph edges and authored evidence are checked; missing provenance or unsupported patterns hold. Neither extra semantic edges nor answer constants inferred from expected results are admitted. Semantic graph constants, input-span copies and graph node identities are different typed constructs. Native program event/node symbols are question variables, and passage IDs never leak into patterns. `anchor` is provenance only. The experiment never edits the accepted reader, native engine, or private evaluations, and no grammar is promoted to the main model.

A candidate's success is conditional on search space, annotations and held-out coverage; it is not a claim of universal language understanding or global optimality. Any incomplete implementation or missing checker must fail closed.

Implemented pilot API: `v2.induce(train_examples)` enumerates bounded candidates from train alone. `v2.acquire_incremental(train, dev, pairs)` tries single/two-production bundles and bounded union dependency closures by cost, requires a new training derivation and newly passing predicted native dev pair, rejects any ambiguity, full-graph mismatch, native failure or prior dev regression, and records decisions and derivations. `v2.parse(text, grammar)` retains all distinct graphs and reports unresolved, ambiguous, or bounded hold. The final `v2.admit` checks all training and dev graphs, four distinct category-labeled pairs, and actual predicted native executions. The driver uses this incremental path. Selection uses dev as a validation set, so an additional untouched held-out test is needed for any generalization claim. The original v1 `acquire.py` remains an earlier, permanently fail-closed prototype; it is not the v2 admission path.

The learned reusable patch is deliberately narrow: a pair of examples must differ in exactly one fixed text gap and one semantic-literal edge or one inserted/deleted edge, while retaining the same node structure, projection and common-edge evidence alignment. A parser applies at most two patches from the bounded learned inventory; candidate templates, patches, variants and matches have explicit limits. Only anchors explicitly used as `value` input-copy terms can generalize, and then only where at least two distinct fillers were observed for the same template. Predicate, question-word and other anchors remain finite observed surface alternatives. This is not a general graph grammar: multi-edge rewrites, nested syntax, unsupported variable-length interior offsets, overlapping anchors, more than eight nodes, empty-answer native checks and heterogeneous node-order anti-unification currently hold or fail. Training currently requires all `edge_spans`, even though the data format permits omission. The `value` predicate is the explicit v2 structural convention for surface decoding; it must point to an input copy, never a semantic literal.

Every native row has exact author-supplied evidence spans; node-type metadata is justified by its authored anchor or incident annotated edges. Source and negation are checked as ordinary, exact graph predicates/literals, not inferred from absence. Native row `scope_kind=direct_text` attributes the transport row to the annotated utterance carrier and does not assert that the represented event happened directly; a reported source must be encoded by its actual graph edges and explicitly constrained by the question. This does not validate real-world truth or rendered language. Four distinct passing pairs with independently authored category labels are required, but the generic checker cannot establish that those labels truthfully characterize the perturbations. Teacher contrast design and review remain required.

The incremental search is greedy and tries ordinary bundles of at most two productions, plus a union with its missing child rules and at most one additional question or other rule. Candidate ordering has a fixed proposal and round bound; the minimum cost applies only to the checked proposal prefix at each step. A candidate with no newly passing dev pair is rejected, so useful rules can remain unresolved even if they would help only in larger combinations. Truncation holds final admission. These conservative limitations are part of the reported result, not model failure claims.

Before decoding an answer, native checking also searches the non-projected context, checks each context for a projected-role witness through the same native executor, and checks each structural projected node has exactly one copied value. A missing role, missing/ambiguous value, incomplete witness, unsupported frontier or loss of a structural candidate during value projection holds the pair. This prevents a relevant incomplete competitor from disappearing before answer uniqueness is assessed. Results are graph tuples and evidence checks, not natural-language answers.

Final admission requires a separate independently authored contrast manifest: person changes copied values; role swaps two declared edge predicates; negation changes a declared semantic literal; source changes a declared node reference, copy or literal. Same-question pairs must produce different native tuples and both must gain additional proven tuples after the declared query constraint is removed. Changed-question negation/source pairs may alternatively use both crossed native no-evidence controls and literal-only restoration probes. Both linked passage/question pairs must themselves pass predicted-graph native checks. A missing contrast never becomes a pass. Category and predicate interpretation remains an independent author-review obligation.

Parser completeness and candidate-proposal search completeness are independent: a bounded search may leave a usable grammar whose parse is complete while the minimum candidate cost remains unknown. The driver accepts explicit positive --max-proposals and --max-rounds bounds.

Two-child productions are learned only from at least two distinct training parents whose grammar-derived proper prefix/suffix children form an exact, disjoint graph union after span shifts and ID renaming. A separator is copied from training; child and union counts and comparisons are bounded. The operation cannot assert shared-entity identity or merge same-surface nodes, and may not capture nested or linked structures. Open copied values that contain a complete independently derivable incompatible-type child graph with a non-copy operator and relation invalidate that candidate with proof; a lexical substring alone does not prohibit a name or suppress an otherwise valid graph.
